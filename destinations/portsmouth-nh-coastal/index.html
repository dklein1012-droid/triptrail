About Portsmouth, NH

Portsmouth is New England's prettiest small city — 400 years of history on brick sidewalks, a working waterfront, and a food scene that punches way above its weight. Strawbery Banke brings history to life, the submarine thrills kids, and Prescott Park's free gardens are perfect for downtime. An easy two days from Boston. Portsmouth, NH guide

Portsmouth is one of America's oldest seaports, a compact brick city on the Piscataqua River where nearly four centuries of history sit inside a downtown you can walk end to end in fifteen minutes. Ship captains' mansions, working tugboats, and a navy yard across the river in Maine share the waterfront with bookshops, brewpubs, and some of the best eating on the New Hampshire coast. It is a history trip that never feels like homework, which makes it a sneaky-great family destination.

The anchor sight is Strawbery Banke, an outdoor museum of more than thirty restored buildings where costumed role-players cook, garden, and run shops as residents did across three hundred years, and kids can try the trades hands-on. Across town, the USS Albacore, a retired research submarine parked on dry land, lets you squeeze through hatches and bunks of a real Cold War boat. Fill the gaps with Prescott Park's free gardens and summer concerts on the river, and, if the season is right, a whale watch out of Rye Harbor, where the boats head for the feeding grounds of Jeffreys Ledge.

Portsmouth suits families with kids five and up and couples who love a walkable, food-forward small city. Two days covers the museum, the submarine, and downtown with time to linger, and the compact layout means almost no driving once you park. Summer and early fall are prime. If you have extra hours, the coast north and south of town hides beaches and forts enough to stretch the trip another day.

Attractions in Portsmouth, NH

Strawbery Banke Museum

~$24/adult, $12/child (est.)

Ten acres of restored 17th- to 19th-century houses, shops, and gardens in the South End — costumed roleplayers, traditional crafts, and heirloom gardens. Kids love the hands-on activities and the chance to 'work' in the old shops.

USS Albacore Museum

~$10/adult, $5/child (est.)

The 1953 diesel submarine, now a museum you can climb through — bunks, torpedoes, the control room. Kids are mesmerized by the cramped quarters. Self-guided audio tour included; allow an hour.

Prescott Park

Free (est.)

Ten acres of waterfront gardens on the Piscataqua River — free, gorgeous, and perfect for a picnic. Summer brings free concerts and the famous tulip display in spring. Kids run the lawns while parents enjoy the river views.

Granite State Whale Watch

~$60/adult, $35/child (est.)

New Hampshire's only full-time whale watch, sailing from nearby Rye Harbor to Jeffreys Ledge. Naturalist-led 4–5 hour trips with strong humpback, finback, and minke sighting rates, May through October.
