About Keene, NH

Keene is the Monadnock Region's hub — a classic college town (Keene State) with a lively downtown, set beneath Mount Monadnock, the most-climbed mountain in the world. It's an affordable, uncrowded base for hiking, apple picking, and small-town exploring. Fall is peak; summer is relaxed and green. Keene, NH guide

Keene is the small-city heart of New Hampshire's Monadnock Region, a classic New England downtown of brick storefronts and a wide Main Street, all in the shadow of the mountain that gives the region its name. Mount Monadnock is famous in hiking circles as one of the most climbed mountains in the world, and its bare granite summit, visible from all over town, has a way of pulling visitors up it. This trip pairs that climb with the gentler pleasures of the valley below.

The mountain is the main event, and it deserves a full morning. Trails like the White Dot and White Cross form a classic loop, climbing from forest to open ledge until the view opens across three states, with Boston's skyline visible on the clearest days. The hike is a real workout but involves no technical climbing, and sturdy kids manage it happily with breaks. Back in town, the Cheshire Children's Museum gives younger kids a hands-on reset, and the Cathedral of the Pines in nearby Rindge offers peaceful gardens and an outdoor altar framed perfectly on Monadnock itself.

Keene suits active families and hikers who prefer a friendly small city to a resort scene, with the climb best for ages six and up. Start the mountain early on weekends, because trailhead parking fills and the summit gets sociable by midday. In fall, add an orchard stop for apple picking in the valley, and in any season save evening energy for Main Street, where the diners, pubs, and the restored Colonial Theatre give the day a proper finish.

Attractions in Keene, NH

Mount Monadnock

~$15/vehicle day use (est.)

The most-climbed mountain in the world, right in Keene's backyard. The 2.2-mile White Dot Trail to the 3,165-foot bare summit is a classic family challenge — big views for moderate effort. The state park has picnic areas and a visitor center.

Cheshire Children's Museum

~$10/person (est.)

Keene's hands-on children's museum with a miniature supermarket, food truck, construction zone, and a real airplane in the transportation center. Aimed at ages 1–9 — the perfect energy-burner for little kids.

Cathedral of the Pines

Free (est.)

A 128-acre open-air memorial in nearby Rindge with stunning views of Mount Monadnock. The Altar of the Nations, the Women's Memorial Bell Tower, and the gardens make for a peaceful, moving walk. Free and family-friendly.

Alyson's Orchard

Free entry; pick-your-own priced by the bag (est.)

A 450-acre working orchard in Walpole with 50+ apple varieties, pick-your-own in season, a farm stand with cider and pies, and a playground. The views across the Connecticut River Valley are spectacular in fall.
