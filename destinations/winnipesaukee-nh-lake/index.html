About Lake Winnipesaukee, NH

Lake Winnipesaukee is New Hampshire's summer playground — 72 square miles of clear water ringed by mountains, with 250+ islands and a shoreline full of beaches, arcades, and ice cream stands. The M/S Mount Washington cruise is the signature experience; Weirs Beach is the classic honky-tonk; Meredith is the polished waterfront town. Summer is the season — most attractions run June through September. All prices and hours below are estimates — verify before you go, as seasonal schedules change.

Lake Winnipesaukee, NH guide

Lake Winnipesaukee is New Hampshire's big water: over forty thousand acres, hundreds of islands, and a shoreline of resort towns that have hosted summer vacations since the steamboat era. Each town has its own flavor, from the boardwalk buzz of Weirs Beach to the quiet marinas of Meredith, and the lake is large enough that choosing where to base yourself shapes the whole trip. This itinerary splits the difference, mixing classic amusements with slow lake time.

The essential ritual is getting out on the water, and the M/S Mount Washington, a grand excursion boat that has cruised these waters in various forms for over a century, is the way to do it, looping past island cottages and mountain views with meals and music aboard. On shore, Weirs Beach delivers the old-school boardwalk package of arcades, a public beach, and the drive-in, while Funspot nearby claims the title of the world's largest arcade, with floors of classic cabinets and bowling. For the wild counterpoint, the Squam Lakes Natural Science Center shows native bears, otters, and mountain lions in forested enclosures near the quieter lake next door.

Winnipesaukee suits lake people: families who swim, boaters, and anyone whose ideal day ends with sunset over water. It is a summer destination through and through, best from late June through early September, and ideal for ages four and up. Because the lake is big, cluster your days by shore, one for the Weirs and Laconia side, one for Meredith and the science center, rather than crisscrossing the same water all weekend.

Attractions in Lake Winnipesaukee, NH

M/S Mount Washington Cruise

~$45/adult, $22/child scenic cruise (est.)

The 230-foot M/S Mount Washington cruises the lake from Weirs Beach, with 2.5-hour scenic daytime cruises and sunset dinner dances. The mailboat run — delivering mail to island homes — is the classic family pick.

Weirs Beach

Free beach; parking ~$15/day (est.)

The lake's classic honky-tonk beach village — boardwalk, arcades, the historic drive-in theater, and the M/S Mount Washington dock. Swim at the public beach, play the arcades, and eat ice cream on the boardwalk.

Funspot

Free entry; games ~$20–$40 in tokens (est.)

The world's largest arcade in Laconia — 500+ classic and modern games, plus bowling, mini-golf, and bingo. A shrine to pinball and Pac-Man that kids and nostalgic parents both love. Free entry; games take tokens.

Squam Lakes Natural Science Center

~$24/adult, $18/child (est.)

A 200-acre wildlife park in Holderness where native animals — black bears, mountain lions, otters — live in natural habitats along accessible trails. The otter feeding and the bear talk are kid favorites. Allow 3 hours.
