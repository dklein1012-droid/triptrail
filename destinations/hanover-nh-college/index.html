About Hanover, NH

Hanover is the Ivy League town that feels like a village — Dartmouth's beautiful campus, the Connecticut River, and one of New England's best children's science museums just across the bridge in Vermont. It's a cultured, easy two days: world-class museums, sculpture gardens, and swimming holes. Fall is gorgeous; summer is quiet and relaxed. Hanover, NH guide

Hanover is the Upper Valley's polished college town, home to Dartmouth College and the kind of main street that seems arranged for a brochure: brick sidewalks, a beloved bookstore, the Hanover Inn facing the Green. But this trip is not just a campus tour. The Connecticut River runs along the town's edge, a top-tier children's science museum sits ten minutes away, and one of America's finest sculpture gardens hides in the hills of Cornish. Hanover works as the cultured base for a surprisingly outdoorsy weekend.

Start with the campus itself: the Green, Baker Library's tower, and the Hood Museum of Art, whose free galleries range from ancient Assyrian reliefs to contemporary work. Then give the kids their due at the Montshire Museum of Science in Norwich, just across the river in Vermont, where more than a hundred hands-on exhibits sprawl indoors and out across a hundred acres of trails and water play. The trip's quiet masterpiece is the Saint-Gaudens National Historical Park, the summer estate of the sculptor Augustus Saint-Gaudens, where monumental bronzes, including the Shaw Memorial figures, stand in gardens with mountain views.

Hanover suits families with curious kids five and up, alumni, and travelers who like a town with brains. Two days is comfortable: one for campus and museums, one for the river and Saint-Gaudens. The Connecticut River offers swimming spots, kayak rentals, and flat trails when you need a nature break from the galleries. Fall weekends are the busiest and the prettiest; book rooms early if a Dartmouth home game is on the calendar.

Attractions in Hanover, NH

Hood Museum of Art

Free (est.)

Dartmouth's art museum, free and open to all, with strong collections of American, European, and Native American art. The building itself is beautiful, and the rotating exhibitions are consistently excellent. A quick, rewarding hour.

Montshire Museum of Science

~$19/adult, $16/child (est.)

Across the river in Norwich, VT, this beloved science museum has 100+ hands-on exhibits, live animals, and the famous 110-foot leaf-cutter ant colony. The outdoor science park is great in summer. A top-tier kids' museum.

Dartmouth College Campus

Free (est.)

One of America's prettiest college campuses — the Green, Baker Tower, and the Hopkins Center. Walk the campus, visit the Rauner Special Collections Library, and grab a meal in town. The bookstore is a great souvenir stop.

Saint-Gaudens National Historical Park

~$10/adult (est.)

The sculptor's Cornish estate — 150 acres of gardens, studios, and 100+ works including the famous Shaw Memorial. Kids love the bronze sculptures and the big lawns; the Sunday concerts in summer are magical.
