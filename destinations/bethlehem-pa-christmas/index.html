Bethlehem, PA guide

Bethlehem calls itself Christmas City USA, and it is not a nickname the town wears lightly. For six weekends from mid-November to December 20, this steel town on the Lehigh River turns its two historic halves into one holiday walk: a German-style market glowing at the foot of five rusted blast furnaces on the South Side, and wooden vendor huts along the Moravian district's brick sidewalks across the river. It is the rare Christmas destination that feels earned, because the history underneath it is real.

A good Bethlehem weekend splits its time between the two sides of the river. Start south at SteelStacks, where the Hoover-Mason Trestle lifts you forty-six feet up for a half-mile walk past the furnaces, then step inside the National Museum of Industrial History to see the machines that built it. Come back after dark for Christkindlmarkt, when the market lights come on against the furnaces and roasted nuts and mulled cider fill PNC Plaza. Cross the river on day two for the Moravian side: a tour of the 1741 Gemeinhaus, the oldest building in town and part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site, then the free Christmas City Village huts in the Sun Inn Courtyard and a stroll down Main Street.

Bethlehem suits families who want a Christmas trip without the crowds and prices of the big-city markets, and it is at its best on a Friday-to-Sunday weekend, when both markets are open. Book Christkindlmarkt tickets ahead; weekend slots sell out, and Friday evenings after 5 p.m. are free. Dress for standing outside and save room for dinner downtown, where the old taverns and bistros of Main Street and Broad Street are part of it.

Attractions in Bethlehem, PA

Christkindlmarkt Bethlehem

~$14-$16.50/adult, kids 5 and under free (est.)

ArtsQuest's German-style holiday market sets up at PNC Plaza beneath the five blast furnaces, with artisan huts, live music, ice carving, and food stalls. The market's lights against the furnaces after dark are the trip's signature sight.

Hoover-Mason Trestle

Free (est.)

A half-mile elevated walkway, 46 feet up, running right past the five blast furnaces of the old Bethlehem Steel plant. Interpretive signs and quotes from former steelworkers tell the story of the mill as you walk.

National Museum of Industrial History

~$15/adult, $10/child (est.)

A Smithsonian affiliate housed in the former Electrical Repair Shop of the Bethlehem Steel plant, with more than 200 artifacts of American industry, a working steam engine, and guided tours of the Hoover-Mason Trestle.

Moravian Museum of Bethlehem

~$20/adult, $8/child tour (est.)

Housed in the 1741 Gemeinhaus, the oldest building in Bethlehem, the museum tells the story of the Moravian settlers who founded the town. Docent-led tours cover the community's crafts, medicine, music, and schools, and the complex is part of the Moravian Church Settlements UNESCO World Heritage Site.
