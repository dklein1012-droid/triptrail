About Philadelphia, PA

Philadelphia is the rare American city where the history is the headline act and the food scene keeps pace. Two days cover the essential founding story — Independence Hall, the Liberty Bell, the Revolution museum — plus a world-class art museum, a legendary food hall, and the cheesesteak pilgrimage. Center City's walkability means you see more and commute less.

Philadelphia, PA guide

Philadelphia rewards visitors who like their history walkable and their meals memorable. The city's founding-era core is remarkably intact: within a few blocks you can stand where the Declaration of Independence was debated, see the Liberty Bell, and tour the museum devoted to the Revolution those rooms set in motion. But Philly is no open-air museum. It is a living, opinionated city of neighborhoods, murals, and fiercely defended food traditions, and two days is enough to feel both sides of it.

Give the first day to the historic mile: Independence Hall, the Liberty Bell Center, and the Museum of the American Revolution make a natural sequence, with Reading Terminal Market, the great 1893 food hall, waiting a few blocks away for lunch. Graze there from roast pork sandwiches to Amish baked goods. Day two can tilt toward art and atmosphere: the Philadelphia Museum of Art for the collections and the famous steps out front, the Barnes Foundation for one of the world's great private art collections, or Eastern State Penitentiary, where crumbling cellblocks tell a stranger, sadder story of American justice.

Philadelphia suits nearly everyone, from families with kids who will love the Franklin Institute's hands-on science to couples on a food-focused weekend. The Center City grid is flat and walkable, and the historic sights cluster tightly, so you will spend your time seeing things rather than commuting. Spring and fall bring the best walking weather. And settle the cheesesteak question properly: try a classic at Pat's or Geno's if you must, then form your own opinion at John's Roast Pork.

Attractions in Philadelphia, PA

Independence Hall + Liberty Bell

Day 1 · Morning · 9:00 AM – 12:30 PM · Free, timed tickets required for Independence Hall (est.)

Start at Independence National Historical Park: the Liberty Bell Center (free, no tickets, ~30 minutes — go early to beat lines) then Independence Hall, where timed entry tickets are free but must be reserved. The guided tour covers the Assembly Room where the Declaration and Constitution were signed (~45 minutes). Walk 5 minutes to Congress Hall and the President's House memorial to round out the morning.

Museum of the American Revolution + Old City

Day 1 · Afternoon · 1:00 PM – 5:00 PM · Museum ~$25/adult (est.)

Walk 5 minutes to the Museum of the American Revolution for Washington's actual headquarters tent and immersive, story-driven galleries (allow ~2.5 hours — it's the best history museum in the city). Then wander Elfreth's Alley, the nation's oldest continuously inhabited residential street (free, 15 minutes), and browse the shops and galleries of Old City. Everything this afternoon is on foot within a few blocks.

Dinner in Old City / Society Hill

Day 1 · Evening · 6:00 PM – 9:00 PM · ~$70–$110 for two (est.)

Dinner in Old City or Society Hill — Zahav's modern Israeli tasting menu (book weeks ahead) is the splurge pick; the surrounding blocks are full of excellent bistros and wine bars for a more casual night. After dinner, walk the cobblestones of Head House Square or catch the twinkle of the historic streetlamps along Elfreth's Alley.

Philadelphia Museum of Art + Reading Terminal Market

Day 2 · Morning · 9:00 AM – 1:00 PM · Art Museum ~$30/adult (est.)

Start at the Philadelphia Museum of Art — run the 72 Rocky Steps for the classic photo, then go inside for one of America's great art collections (allow ~2.5 hours; the Perelman Building next door covers modern design). Then take a 10-minute subway/taxi ride to Reading Terminal Market, the 1893 food hall under the old train shed, for lunch grazing: DiNic's roast pork, Beiler's donuts, Amish baked goods (~1 hour).

Franklin Institute or Eastern State Penitentiary

Day 2 · Afternoon · 1:30 PM – 4:30 PM · Museum ~$25/adult (est.); lunch ~$30 for two (est.)

Take the subway or a 10-minute ride to the Franklin Institute for hands-on science exhibits including the giant walk-through Heart (~2 hours, great with kids). Alternatively, tour the haunting cellblocks of Eastern State Penitentiary, a 15-minute walk north, with Steve Buscemi's audio narration (~1.5 hours). Both are substantial; pick one rather than rushing both.

Cheesesteak pilgrimage in South Philly

Day 2 · Evening · 5:30 PM – 8:00 PM · ~$15/person (est.)

Settle the cheesesteak question: South Philly's Pat's King of Steaks and Geno's Steaks face off across Passyunk Avenue (10-minute drive or subway ride from Center City) — order 'wit' or 'witout' like a local. For the connoisseur's pick, John's Roast Pork earns consistent best-in-city honors. End the night with Federal Donuts' hot fresh donuts and fried chicken for the road.
