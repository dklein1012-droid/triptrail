About Brandywine Valley, PA

The Brandywine Valley packs four of the Northeast's great holiday shows into a few country miles: Longwood Gardens' half-million-light Christmas, Yuletide at the Winterthur mansion, the Brandywine Railroad at the museum in Chadds Ford, and Holidays at Hagley along the river. This two-day plan is anchored in Kennett Square, Pennsylvania, with two stops a short drive across the Delaware line. Brandywine Valley, PA guide

Every December the Brandywine Valley turns into the Northeast's most concentrated Christmas show. It starts in Kennett Square, where Longwood Gardens strings more than half a million lights through its gardens and conservatories, and it spreads a few country miles in every direction: a Gilded Age mansion dressed room by room for Yuletide, a model railroad that has run every holiday since 1972, and a riverside powder mill dressed for the season. The du Pont family built this valley's gardens and estates, and at Christmas the whole place glows like it was made for it.

A good Brandywine weekend runs on a simple rhythm: gardens by day, lights after dark. Start in Chadds Ford at the Brandywine Museum of Art, where the Wyeth paintings share the floor with the Brandywine Railroad's two thousand feet of miniature track, then spend the afternoon in Kennett Square, the mushroom capital, before Longwood opens its evening hours and the lights come on. Day two belongs to the mansions: Winterthur's Yuletide tour winds through rooms dressed after storybook tales, and Hagley, the du Ponts' first American home and powder yard, finishes the trip along the river. Two stops sit just across the Delaware line, a ten-minute drive from Chadds Ford.

Brandywine suits families who want a Christmas trip built around wonder rather than shopping, and it is at its best on a Friday-to-Sunday weekend from late November, when all four holiday shows overlap. Book Longwood's timed tickets and Winterthur reservations before you leave home; both sell out on December weekends. Dress for walking between warm conservatories and cold garden paths, and save dinner for Kennett Square, where the farm-to-table bistros are part of the trip.

Attractions in Brandywine Valley, PA

Longwood Gardens

~$45-$52/adult, $25-$32/youth (est.)

One of the world's great gardens, 1,100 acres of conservatories, fountains, and woodland near Kennett Square. A Longwood Christmas fills it with more than half a million lights, 100 decorated trees, a model Garden Railway, and three treehouses dressed for the season.

Winterthur Museum, Garden & Library

~$33/adult, $12/child (est.)

Henry Francis du Pont's 175-room country estate, now a museum of American decorative arts. Yuletide dresses the house room by room after beloved stories, with a giant gingerbread house, an 18-room dollhouse, and the Enchanted Woods children's garden outside.

Brandywine Museum of Art

~$25/adult (est.)

A converted 19th-century gristmill on the Brandywine River, home to three generations of Wyeth paintings and the studios where they worked. At the holidays the Brandywine Railroad takes over a gallery: model trains on 2,000 feet of track, running since 1972.

Hagley Museum and Library

~$22/adult, kids 14 and under free (est.)

The du Pont family's first American home and the black powder mills that started their fortune, set along the Brandywine. Holidays at Hagley dresses the residence for the season, with mill demonstrations, Santa visits, and free admission for kids 14 and under.
