About Bucks County, PA

Bucks County packs a full Christmas season into a few country miles: a million lights and a gingerbread contest at Peddler's Village, A Very Furry Christmas at Sesame Place, the holiday castles of Doylestown, and the 250th anniversary of Washington's crossing on the Delaware. This two-day plan is anchored in New Hope and Lahaska, about 90 minutes from New York City. All prices and hours below are estimates — verify before you go, as holiday dates and hours are set by season.

Bucks County, PA guide

Every December, Bucks County turns its whole map into a Christmas card. It starts in Lahaska, where Peddler's Village strings a million lights over brick walkways and fills its event center with a hundred gingerbread houses, and it spreads outward: Elmo and Cookie Monster under millions of lights in Langhorne, a concrete castle dressed for the holidays in Doylestown, and the riverbank where Washington crossed on Christmas night 250 years ago. It is about ninety minutes from New York, and at Christmas it feels like another century.

A good Bucks County weekend runs on a simple rhythm: characters by day, lights after dark. Start in Langhorne at Sesame Place, where A Very Furry Christmas fills the park with rides, a parade, and a tree-light show, then drive up to Lahaska for the gingerbread display and village shops before the lights come on. Day two belongs to the county's history: the Mercer Museum and Fonthill Castle, two concrete castles from the same eccentric tilemaker, and Washington Crossing, where the 250th anniversary reenactments run December 13 and Christmas Day. Shady Brook Farm's drive-through light show is the classic nightcap.

Bucks County suits families with young kids best, and it is at its best on a Friday-to-Sunday weekend from late November, when the village lights, the Sesame Place season, and the castle tours all overlap. Book Sesame Place tickets online before you go; the gate price is steep, and Christmas dates sell out. Register in advance for the Washington Crossing reenactments too, since attendance is capped for the 250th anniversary year. Dress warm, keep the car close, and save dinner for New Hope, where the riverside restaurants are part of the trip.

Attractions in Bucks County, PA

Peddler's Village

Free admission

A 42-acre shopping village of brick walkways and colonial-style shops in Lahaska. From November 20, a million lights cover the village, the Gingerbread Competition fills the Visitor & Event Center with more than 100 edible creations, and a 1920s carousel spins on the green. Admission and parking are free.

Sesame Place Philadelphia

~$49–$111/person (est.)

The country's only Sesame Street theme park, in Langhorne, turned into a winter wonderland for A Very Furry Christmas. Millions of lights, kid-sized rides, a Christmas parade, the 1-2-3 Christmas Tree music-and-lights show, and photos with Santa in the Furry Workshop. Book online — gate tickets run about $111.

Mercer Museum & Fonthill Castle

~$20/adult, $10/youth, each site (est.)

Two concrete castles in Doylestown built by tilemaker Henry Chapman Mercer: the six-story Mercer Museum, packed with 40,000 pre-industrial tools, and Fonthill Castle, his tile-covered home, open by guided tour. Holiday tours run November 14 to December 22, with evening tours and a Holiday Lights Meander in late November.

Washington Crossing Historic Park

Free to visit; Dec 13 reenactment ~$10/adult (est.)

The Pennsylvania shore where Washington's army crossed the Delaware on Christmas night, 1776. Walk the riverbank and the historic village, and catch the 250th anniversary reenactments: the First Crossing on December 13 and the Christmas Day Crossing on December 25, 2026. Advance registration is required for both in the anniversary year.
