About Cape May, NJ

Cape May is America's original seaside resort — a town of candy-colored Victorian mansions at the very tip of New Jersey, where dolphins cruise past 19th-century lighthouses. Three days gives you the full arc: Victorian history, beach time, a bay crossing, and a free zoo. All prices and hours below are estimates — verify before you go, as many attractions run seasonally.

Cape May, NJ guide

Cape May is the Jersey Shore's grand old lady, a whole town designated a National Historic Landmark for its dense collection of Victorian houses painted in lollipop colors. Families have summered here since the 1800s, and the town still runs on porch swings, trolley tours, and dinners in converted mansions. What sets Cape May apart from other shore towns is how much there is to do beyond the beach, which matters on the third day of any family vacation.

Anchor the trip with the town's icons: the 1859 lighthouse at Cape May Point, the Emlen Physick Estate for a window into Victorian life, and Sunset Beach, where the hulking wreck of the concrete ship Atlantus sits offshore and the evening sky does the rest. Then layer in the wild side. Cape May is one of North America's great bird migration crossroads, dolphins cruise just off the beaches, and the free county zoo is genuinely one of the best small zoos in the region. The ferry to Lewes, Delaware makes an easy adventure day if you want one.

Cape May suits all ages, from toddlers happy in the calm surf to grandparents who appreciate the architecture and the trolley commentary. Three days is the sweet spot, enough for beach time plus the lighthouse, zoo, and a boat ride without rushing. Visit in September if you can: the ocean is still warm, the crowds thin out, and the fall hawk migration turns the sky over the Point into a show of its own.

Attractions in Cape May, NJ

Emlen Physick Estate

Day 1 · Morning · 10:00 AM – 12:00 PM · $15/adult, $8/child (est.)

Tour the 1879 Stick-style mansion of Cape May's most famous Victorian family — the 45-minute guided tour is full of period detail and local lore. Book the 10:30 AM tour to keep the afternoon free.

Washington Street Mall

Day 1 · Afternoon · 12:30 PM – 3:00 PM · Free to stroll (est.)

Walk three pedestrian-only blocks of Victorian storefronts — fudge shops, bookstores, and galleries. Grab lunch at one of the mall's cafés, then rent bikes (many shops along the mall) for the rest of your stay; Cape May is flat and bike-friendly.

Sunset Beach & the SS Atlantus

Day 1 · Evening · 5:30 PM – 8:00 PM · Free (est.)

Drive 10 minutes west to Sunset Beach. Arrive an hour before sunset to hunt for Cape May diamonds (polished quartz pebbles), then watch the sun drop behind the rusting hulk of the SS Atlantus, an experimental concrete shipwrecked in 1926.

Cape May Lighthouse & State Park

Day 2 · Morning · 9:00 AM – 12:00 PM · $12/adult, $8/child (est.)

Drive 10 minutes to Cape May Point State Park and climb the lighthouse's 199 cast-iron steps for a sweeping view of the Delaware Bay meeting the Atlantic. Afterwards, walk the park's short red trail through freshwater wetlands — prime birdwatching.

Cape May Beach

Day 2 · Afternoon · 12:30 PM – 4:00 PM · Beach tags ~$10/day adult, under 12 free (est.)

Claim a spot on Cape May's guarded beaches — the sand is wide and the surf gentle. Beach tags are required (buy at tag booths on the beach entrances); lifeguards are on duty roughly 10 AM–5:30 PM in season.

Dolphin & sunset cruise

Day 2 · Evening · 5:00 PM – 7:30 PM · $55/adult, $35/child (est.)

Board at the Miss Chris Marina for a 2-hour sunset cruise — Cape May is one of the East Coast's best dolphin-watching spots, and humpback sightings have grown common in recent years. Bring a jacket; it gets breezy on the water.

Cape May–Lewes Ferry

Day 3 · Morning · 8:30 AM – 11:30 AM · ~$20/adult foot passenger round-trip (est.)

Take the 8:45 AM sailing for the 85-minute crossing to Lewes, Delaware — dolphins often ride the bow wake. Foot passengers can simply ride round-trip without disembarking; the return lands you back in Cape May by late morning.

Cape May County Park & Zoo

Day 3 · Afternoon · 1:00 PM – 4:00 PM · Free admission; $5 parking (est.)

Drive 20 minutes north to this 85-acre accredited zoo — lions, giraffes, and a reptile house, all free. Two to three hours is plenty, and the playground next door burns off any remaining kid energy.

Farewell dinner on the Washington Street Mall

Day 3 · Evening · 5:30 PM – 8:00 PM · Dinner ~$20–40/person (est.)

Back downtown for a farewell dinner, then one last evening stroll down Columbia Avenue to admire the painted Victorian 'ladies' — Congress Hall's lawn is a fine spot for a final family photo.
