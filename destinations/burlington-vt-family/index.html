About Burlington, VT

Burlington is Vermont's biggest little city — a walkable college town on the eastern shore of Lake Champlain with mountain views in every direction. The lakefront bike path, the hands-on ECHO science center, and the car-free Church Street make it an easy, relaxing family base. Fall foliage is spectacular; summer brings swimming and cruises. Burlington, VT guide

Burlington is Vermont's largest city, which still makes it a small, friendly lakeside town where the main street is a brick pedestrian mall and the sunset faces west over Lake Champlain toward the Adirondack skyline. It has a college town's energy, a working waterfront, and a food scene that punches far above its size, from farm-to-table dining rooms to the humble creemee, Vermont's beloved maple soft serve. Three days here feels relaxed rather than rushed, and that is the point.

Build the trip around the lake and the town's two great family museums. The ECHO Leahy Center pairs aquarium touch tanks and lake ecology exhibits with rooftop views of the water, while the Shelburne Museum, a short drive south, spreads dozens of historic buildings, a steamboat, and folk art across a park-like campus that kids can explore for hours. Between museum days, the waterfront path and bike trail run for miles along the shore, Church Street handles shopping and street performers, and a sunset cruise shows you the city from its best angle.

Burlington suits families who want a town base with real amenities plus easy nature, and it works from late spring through foliage season. Summer brings swimming and sailing weather, while September and early October add fall color in the hills behind town. Rent bikes if you can: the flat waterfront trail is the city's backyard, and a morning ride to find creemees is a small ritual worth adopting.

Attractions in Burlington, VT

ECHO, Leahy Center for Lake Champlain

~$19.50/adult, $16/child (est.)

Vermont's beloved lake aquarium and science center right on the Burlington waterfront. Touch tanks, the live frog collection, and the shipwreck theater keep kids busy for hours. The rooftop lake views are a bonus.

Shelburne Museum

~$28/adult, $14/child (est.)

A 45-acre campus of historic New England buildings — a round barn, a one-room schoolhouse, a steamboat — filled with folk art and Americana. Kids get a passport to stamp at each building; the playground and carousel are big hits.

Church Street Marketplace

Free to stroll (est.)

Four blocks of car-free brick street packed with shops, street performers, and snack stops — the beating heart of Burlington. Kids love the performers and the Ben & Jerry's scoop shop; parents love the people-watching.

Spirit of Ethan Allen Cruise

~$32/adult, $16/child scenic cruise (est.)

Burlington's big triple-decker cruise ship offering narrated scenic and sunset cruises on Lake Champlain. The 90-minute daytime cruise is perfect for kids — snacks onboard, room to move, and mountain views the whole way.
