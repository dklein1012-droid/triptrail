About Manchester, VT

Manchester pairs Green Mountain scenery with more to do than you'd expect — Lincoln's Hildene estate, Bromley's summer adventure park, and a surprisingly good arts scene. The famous outlets give parents a bonus, and the mountain views are everywhere. It's an easy two-day trip or a stop between Bennington and the northern Greens. Manchester, VT guide

Manchester sits where Vermont's marble valley meets the Green Mountains, and it has been a resort town since the railroad era, when grand hotels lined its streets and visitors came for the mountain air. The modern version balances three personalities: a gracious historic village, a genuine outdoor base for the surrounding peaks and rivers, and a famous strip of outlet stores along Route 7A. This trip deliberately samples all three, because each explains a different era of the town.

The essential visit is Hildene, the Georgian Revival estate built by Robert Todd Lincoln, the president's son, who summered here for decades. The house, formal gardens, and working farm sit on a shelf above the Batten Kill valley with one of the great views in southern Vermont. For energy, Bromley Mountain runs an alpine slide and adventure park in summer and family-friendly skiing in winter, while the Southern Vermont Arts Center spreads sculpture across its hillside grounds back in town. Anglers should not miss the American Museum of Fly Fishing, since the Batten Kill is one of the storied trout streams of the East.

Manchester suits travelers who mix culture, outdoors, and a little shopping in one weekend, and it works in both green and ski seasons. Families with kids five and up will get the most from Bromley and the estate farm. Give Hildene a full morning rather than squeezing it in, and if the outlets are on your list, go on a weekday or first thing in the morning before the parking lots fill.

Attractions in Manchester, VT

Hildene, The Lincoln Family Home

~$25/adult, $8/child (est.)

The Lincoln family's summer estate — 412 acres of formal gardens, a Georgian Revival mansion, and the fascinating exhibit on Robert Todd Lincoln. Kids love the 1,000-foot garden walk and the goat dairy and cheesemaking demos.

Bromley Mountain Adventure Park

~$45/person adventure park (est.)

In summer, Bromley transforms into an adventure park with the famous alpine slide, the Sun Mountain Flyer zip line, giant swing, and scenic chairlift rides. The alpine slide alone is worth the trip — kids ride it again and again.

Southern Vermont Arts Center

~$20/adult, free under 18 (est.)

A lovely regional arts center on 100+ acres with galleries, a sculpture garden, and the Yester House mansion. The summer art camps and family programs are excellent; the grounds are free to wander.

American Museum of Fly Fishing

~$10/adult (est.)

The world's largest museum devoted to fly fishing, with 22,000 flies, 1,400 rods, and fascinating angling history. Even non-fishers enjoy the craftsmanship on display. Small, quick, and right on Main Street.
