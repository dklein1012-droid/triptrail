About Catskills, NY

The Catskills are New York's classic mountain escape — big waterfalls, glassy lakes, and small towns with real character. Day one tackles the famous Kaaterskill Falls trail and cools off at North-South Lake; day two trades hiking boots for a scenic chairlift and a lazy tube float down the Esopus. It's an outdoorsy weekend that still leaves room for diner pancakes.

Catskills, NY guide

The Catskills invented the American mountain vacation. Long before the Adirondacks had hotels, artists were painting these blue ridges and city families were spending whole summers in boarding houses along the creeks. Today's Catskills mix that heritage with a creative revival: waterfall trails and swimming holes by day, farm-to-table dinners and small-town main streets by night. This trip leans into the wild side, with the region's most famous waterfall as its centerpiece.

Kaaterskill Falls is the star, a two-tier cascade dropping over two hundred feet through a rocky amphitheater, reached by a trail that mixes boardwalk, stone steps, and clifftop viewpoints. Pair it with North-South Lake, the old campground in the pines where you can swim, paddle, and walk the escarpment trail to ledges that once held grand hotels, all facing a Hudson Valley panorama. Round out the weekend on the water in Phoenicia, where outfitters rent tubes for the gentle float down Esopus Creek, and on the chairlift at Hunter Mountain, which trades skiers for sightseers in summer.

This trip suits active families and hikers, best for ages six and up, because the signature trails involve steep stairs and exposed edges that demand attention. Go on a weekday if you can, since Kaaterskill's parking fills early on summer weekends, and always stay on marked trails and behind railings near the falls. The reward for the effort is the Catskills at their best: cold creek water, big views, and mountain air two hours from the city.

Attractions in Catskills, NY

Kaaterskill Falls Trail

Day 1 · Morning · 8:30 AM – 12:00 PM · Free (est.)

Hit the Kaaterskill Falls trail early — the 2.6-mile round trip descends 600+ stairs to the base of the 260-foot two-tier falls. Go slow, wear real shoes, and stay on the marked trail; the upper viewing platform alone is worth it if the full descent is too much for the kids.

North-South Lake Campground & Day Use Area

Day 1 · Afternoon · 1:00 PM – 5:00 PM · $10/vehicle (est.)

Drive 15 minutes to North-South Lake for a swim at the sandy beach and a lakeside picnic. Rent a kayak or paddleboard at the concession, or take the easy loop trail to Ashley Falls. The lake sits on the escarpment with big-sky views across the Hudson Valley.

Palenville and Kaaterskill overlook

Day 1 · Evening · 6:00 PM – 8:00 PM · $55/family dinner (est.)

Dinner in Palenville at a cozy market cafe, then drive 10 minutes up Route 23A to the Kaaterskill Falls overlook pull-off for sunset — the clove glows gold and you can hear the falls below.

Hunter Mountain Scenic Skyride

Day 2 · Morning · 9:30 AM – 11:30 AM · $25/adult, $18/child (est.)

Ride the Scenic Skyride chairlift at Hunter Mountain for panoramic views across three states. At the summit, walk the short interpretive loop, then ride back down — or hike down the service trail if legs are fresh. Total time about 2 hours door to door.

Esopus Creek tubing, Phoenicia

Day 2 · Afternoon · 1:00 PM – 4:00 PM · $25/person tubing (est.)

Drive 25 minutes to Phoenicia for a lazy afternoon tubing the Esopus Creek — the outfitter buses you upstream and you float back down through mountain scenery in about 2 hours. Water levels are best June through August.

Phoenicia Diner

Day 2 · Evening · 5:00 PM – 6:30 PM · $50/family dinner (est.)

The Phoenicia Diner is a Catskills institution — farm-to-table comfort food in a converted 1960s railcar diner. Arrive before 6 to beat the weekend dinner rush, then an easy drive home.
