About Finger Lakes, NY

The Finger Lakes distill upstate New York to its essence: glacier-carved lakes, waterfall-laced gorges, and a wine region that rivals anything on the coasts. This weekend pairs the can't-miss gorge hike at Watkins Glen with lakeside wine tasting and Ithaca's waterfall circuit — romantic, scenic, and paced so you actually relax between stops.

Finger Lakes, NY guide

The Finger Lakes region was carved by glaciers into eleven long, deep lakes, and the gorges at their southern ends hold some of the most dramatic scenery in the East. This weekend centers on the two experiences that define the region: walking the gorge trails of Watkins Glen and Taughannock Falls, and tasting wine on the slopes above Seneca Lake, where riesling has made the region famous among American wine lovers. Ithaca and Corning round it out with college-town energy and world-class glass.

Watkins Glen State Park is the headline: a two-mile trail that climbs past nineteen waterfalls, crossing stone bridges and passing behind curtains of falling water as the gorge narrows around you. Taughannock Falls answers with a single plunge taller than Niagara in a vast rock amphitheater. Between hikes, the Seneca Lake Wine Trail strings together dozens of wineries along the lake's eastern and western shores, many of them pioneering family-run estates. In Corning, the Museum of Glass pairs astonishing art with live hot-glass demonstrations where artists pull glowing sculptures from the furnace in front of you.

This trip suits couples, friend groups, and families with kids seven and up who can handle gorge stairs and wet rock. The wine trail means choosing a designated driver or booking a tour, and the gorge trails are at their best on weekday mornings before the tour groups arrive. Come in May for waterfalls at full flow or in October for harvest season and foliage, when the vineyard rows turn gold above the lakes.

Attractions in Finger Lakes, NY

Watkins Glen State Park — Gorge Trail

Day 1 · Morning · 8:30 AM – 12:00 PM · Park entry ~$10/vehicle (est.)

Arrive early at Watkins Glen State Park for the Gorge Trail — 2 miles past 19 waterfalls, stone bridges, and tunnels carved through 200-foot cliffs. The full loop takes 2–3 hours with 800+ steps; start at the main entrance and climb, then descend via the rim trails. Morning light in the gorge is unbeatable and crowds are thinner before 11 AM.

Seneca Lake Wine Trail tastings

Day 1 · Afternoon · 1:30 PM – 5:00 PM · Tastings ~$15–$25/person per winery (est.)

Drive 20 minutes up the west side of Seneca Lake to the Seneca Lake Wine Trail. Start at Dr. Konstantin Frank Winery, the pioneer that proved European vinifera could thrive here, then continue to Hermann J. Wiemer or Ravines Wine Cellars for dry Rieslings with lake views. Plan 2–3 wineries over ~3 hours; designate a driver or book a local tour service. The lakeside drive itself, on Route 14, is half the pleasure.

Dinner in Watkins Glen village

Day 1 · Evening · 6:00 PM – 8:30 PM · ~$90–$130 for two (est.)

Return to Watkins Glen village (15 minutes) for dinner — lakeside tables at spots like the Stonecat Café in nearby Hector offer farm-to-table cooking with sunset views over the water. After dinner, walk the village pier or drive 10 minutes to Clute Memorial Park for a quiet lakeside stroll as the light fades over Seneca Lake.

Taughannock Falls + Buttermilk Falls

Day 2 · Morning · 9:00 AM – 12:30 PM · Park entry ~$10/vehicle (est.)

Drive 35 minutes from Watkins Glen to Taughannock Falls State Park, home to a 215-foot waterfall — taller than Niagara's drop — plunging into a dramatic amphitheater gorge. The overlook is steps from parking; the easy gorge trail to the base takes ~45 minutes round-trip. Continue 15 minutes to Ithaca and visit Buttermilk Falls State Park, where a series of cascades tumbles through a natural swimming hole (swimming seasonal).

Corning Museum of Glass or Cornell Botanic Gardens

Day 2 · Afternoon · 1:30 PM – 4:30 PM · Museum ~$25/adult (est.); parking ~$10 (est.)

Choose your afternoon: drive 40 minutes south to the Corning Museum of Glass for live hot-glass demonstrations and 3,500 years of glass art (~2.5 hours, excellent for kids), or stay in Ithaca for the Cornell Botanic Gardens and a wander through the Ithaca Commons pedestrian mall. Both pair well with a late lunch — Ithaca's food scene punches well above its weight.

Farewell dinner in Ithaca

Day 2 · Evening · 6:00 PM – 8:30 PM · ~$80–$120 for two (est.)

End the weekend in Ithaca with dinner — Maxie's Supper Club for Southern-inspired comfort food, or the Ports Café in Geneva if you're looping back along the lake. If you're staying the night, the sunset from the Taughannock overlook pull-off (10 minutes from Ithaca) is a fitting finale; the falls face west and catch the evening light.
