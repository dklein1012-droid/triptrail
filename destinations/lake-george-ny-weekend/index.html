About Lake George, NY

Lake George has been New York's summer playground since the 1800s, and it still delivers: a 32-mile lake framed by Adirondack peaks, a real sand beach, and enough history and rides to fill a long weekend. This two-day plan mixes lake time with mountain views. All prices and hours below are estimates — verify before you go, as most lake attractions run seasonally.

Lake George, NY guide

Lake George calls itself the Queen of American Lakes, and on a clear morning, with the Adirondack foothills dropping straight into thirty-two miles of island-dotted water, it earns the title. The village at the lake's southern end has been a resort town since the nineteenth century, and it still runs on the classic formula: a sandy beach, a steamboat whistle, an ice cream stand, and mountains in every direction. It is nostalgic without feeling stuck in the past.

A good Lake George weekend mixes water and viewpoints. Swim or picnic at Million Dollar Beach, take a narrated cruise aboard the Minne-Ha-Ha paddlewheel steamboat, and drive the toll road up Prospect Mountain for the summit view over the lake. History threads through it all at Fort William Henry, a reconstructed French and Indian War fort where costumed interpreters fire muskets and explain why this valley mattered so much in the 1750s. Evenings are for the village arcades, mini golf, and a slow walk along Beach Road.

Lake George suits families and couples who want a real lake vacation without a long drive, and it is at its best from late June through early September when the water is swimmable and the boats are running. Summer weekends get busy, so book cruises and lodging ahead and hit the beach early. If you have a third day, the drive north along the lake's quieter eastern shore shows a completely different, wilder side of the same water.

Attractions in Lake George, NY

Million Dollar Beach

Day 1 · Morning · 9:30 AM – 12:30 PM · $10/vehicle parking (est.)

Arrive by 9:30 AM to claim parking and a good patch of sand at this state-run beach on the lake's south end. The swimming area is roped and lifeguarded in summer, with the mountains as your backdrop. Pack a cooler — concessions are basic.

Lake George Steamboat Company — Minne-Ha-Ha

Day 1 · Afternoon · 1:30 PM – 3:30 PM · $30/adult, $15/child (est.)

Walk or drive 5 minutes into Lake George Village for a 1-hour sightseeing cruise aboard an authentic paddlewheeler. The 2:00 PM sailing gives you narration on the lake's mansions and islands without eating the whole afternoon.

Lake George Village & Shepard Park

Day 1 · Evening · 5:00 PM – 9:00 PM · Dinner ~$15–30/person (est.)

Stroll Canada Street's arcades and ice cream shops, then dinner in the village. On summer Thursdays, fireworks launch over the lake around dusk — Shepard Park's beachfront is the prime viewing spot, an easy walk from dinner.

Prospect Mountain Veterans Memorial Highway

Day 2 · Morning · 9:00 AM – 11:30 AM · $10/vehicle (est.)

Drive 5 minutes from the village to the toll road, then wind 5.5 miles to the 2,030-foot summit for a 100-mile view over Lake George and the Green Mountains. A free shuttle covers the last stretch if you'd rather not walk it.

Fort William Henry Museum

Day 2 · Afternoon · 12:30 PM – 2:30 PM · $20/adult, $10/child (est.)

Back down the mountain and 5 minutes into the village for this reconstructed 1755 British fort. Guided tours run hourly with musket and cannon demonstrations the kids will remember. Allow 2 hours.

Six Flags Great Escape, Queensbury

Day 2 · Evening · 4:00 PM – 8:00 PM · Twilight admission ~$40/person (est.)

Drive 15 minutes south to Queensbury for an evening at the theme park — twilight tickets (usually after 4 PM) cost less and the lines shrink. Alternatively, keep it low-key with mini-golf and go-karts along Route 9 back toward the lake.
