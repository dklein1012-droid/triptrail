About Bennington, VT

Bennington is Vermont's history corner — the 1777 battle that turned the Revolution, a grand 19th-century resort era, and Robert Frost's adopted hometown. The monument, the museum's Grandma Moses collection, and the Old First Church all cluster in walkable Old Bennington. It's a quiet, affordable two-day stop with real depth. Bennington, VT guide

Bennington is southern Vermont's history classroom. The town's name echoes through the Revolution thanks to the Battle of Bennington in 1777, a fight actually waged just across the New York line, where American militiamen overwhelmed a British raiding force and helped set up the surrender at Saratoga. The 306-foot stone obelisk raised to commemorate it, the tallest structure in Vermont, still watches over the town, and an elevator ride to its top opens views across three states on a clear day.

The town's second act is artistic. The Bennington Museum holds the largest public collection of paintings by Grandma Moses, the beloved folk artist who began painting in her late seventies on a farm nearby, alongside Revolutionary-era artifacts and a 1760s schoolhouse. In Old Bennington, the white Old First Church shelters the grave of Robert Frost, who chose the spot himself, and the surrounding streets of grand old houses reward a slow walk. A short drive north, the Park-McCullough House offers a preserved Victorian mansion and gardens for a glimpse of the region's prosperous later years.

Bennington suits history-minded travelers and families with kids six and up, and it pairs naturally with a scenic drive: covered bridges dot the surrounding valleys, and the Molly Stark Trail east toward Brattleboro is one of Vermont's prettiest roads. Two easy days cover the monument, museum, and mansion without hurry. Come in late September or early October if you can, when the hills around town are at peak color and the monument view earns every one of its feet.

Attractions in Bennington, VT

Bennington Battle Monument

~$5/adult, $1/child 6–14 (est.)

Vermont's tallest structure — a 306-foot stone obelisk honoring the 1777 Battle of Bennington. Ride the elevator to the observation deck for views into three states. The grounds and gift shop are free; the elevator ticket is a bargain.

Bennington Museum

~$15/adult, $8/child (est.)

A gem of a regional museum with the world's largest collection of Grandma Moses paintings, plus Bennington pottery, early American furniture, and a 1924 Martin Wasp automobile. Small enough for kids to enjoy without museum fatigue.

Park-McCullough Historic Governor's Mansion

~$15/adult tour (est.)

A 36-room Victorian mansion in North Bennington with original furnishings, carriage barn, and gardens. The guided tour brings the Gilded Age to life, and kids love the scale of it all. Seasonal tours — check the schedule.

The Old First Church

Free; donations welcome (est.)

The 1805 white-steepled church in Old Bennington, where poet Robert Frost is buried in the churchyard. The interior is a beautiful example of Federal architecture, and the churchyard stroll is peaceful. Donations welcome.
