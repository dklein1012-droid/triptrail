About Lake Placid, NY

Lake Placid is the only American village to host the Winter Olympics twice, and in December it turns that history into a family weekend: the Holiday Village Stroll, skating where Eric Heiden won five golds, a gondola up Whiteface, and ski jumps towering over the valley. This three-day plan is based on Main Street, about five hours from New York City. All prices and hours below are estimates — verify before you go, as winter dates and hours are set by snow and season.

Lake Placid, NY guide

Lake Placid hosted the Winter Olympics in 1932 and 1980, and the village still runs on that history. Main Street sits between frozen Mirror Lake and the High Peaks, and every December the Holiday Village Stroll fills it with a skating party, Santa photos, a free movie, and a scavenger hunt. It is about five hours from New York City, and in winter it feels like a snow globe that hosted the world.

A good Lake Placid weekend runs on Olympic ice and mountain air. Start at the Olympic Center, where the Miracle on Ice happened: the museum of torches and medals, then an evening skate on the outdoor Speed Skating Oval, where Eric Heiden won five golds. Day two belongs to the mountains: Whiteface, the Olympic ski mountain, for skiing or the Cloudsplitter Gondola to Little Whiteface, then the Olympic Jumping Complex, where a glass elevator climbs 26 stories to the top of the 128-meter jump. Day three ends with High Falls Gorge, a half-mile waterfall walk on the Ausable River, on the drive home.

Lake Placid suits families with kids five and up best, and it is at its best December 11 to 13 for the Holiday Village Stroll, when the skating party, Santa, and the Santa Sunday ski all line up. Whiteface should open November 21, weather permitting, so check whiteface.com before counting on skiing. One honest warning: the Mirror Lake toboggan chute and lake skating track need thick natural ice and usually do not open until January or February, so do not plan a December trip around them. Dress for real Adirondack cold, book lodging early for Stroll weekend, and bring skates.

Attractions in Lake Placid, NY

Holiday Village Stroll

Free to wander

Three days of holiday cheer on Main Street, December 11 to 13: a skating party in the 1932 Rink with free rentals and a visit from Santa, Santa's arrival by firetruck to Mid's Park, photos with Santa, a free movie, a scavenger hunt through the shops, the Jingle Bell Run, and a Santa Sunday ski at Whiteface. The celebration itself is free; food, skating rentals, and parking cost extra.

Lake Placid Olympic Center

~$15/adult (est.)

Home of the 1980 Miracle on Ice: stand in the Herb Brooks Arena where the U.S. hockey team beat the Soviet Union, then visit the Olympic Museum's torches, medals, and Miracle on Ice exhibit, with a bobsled simulator for the kids. Public skating runs in the 1932 Jack Shea Arena. The museum is open daily, 9:30 AM to 5 PM.

Whiteface Mountain

~$30/adult gondola ride (est.)

The 1980 Olympic mountain, with the greatest vertical drop in the East. Skiers get 89 trails and 3,430 feet of vertical; non-skiers ride the Cloudsplitter Gondola from the Main Base Lodge to Little Whiteface in under 15 minutes for the High Peaks view. The season is projected to open November 21, weather permitting, so check whiteface.com before counting on skiing. Lift tickets are dynamically priced, roughly $79 to $134 for adults.

Olympic Jumping Complex

~$20/adult (est.)

Ride a glass-enclosed elevator 26 stories to the top of the 128-meter ski jump, where Olympic jumpers launch, for a 360-degree view of Lake Placid and the Adirondacks. Jumpers train and compete here through the winter, so you may catch practice from the viewing platforms. The Skyride gondola runs seasonally, so check the official site for winter operations before you go.
