Lancaster, PA guide

Lancaster is the heart of Pennsylvania Dutch Country, where horse-drawn buggies share the road with cars and farm stands sell shoofly pie and root beer from the front yard. The county has been home to Amish and Mennonite communities for centuries, and that slower, farm-centered rhythm is exactly what makes it such a restorative family trip. You are not rushing between blockbuster sights here. You are riding a steam train through farmland, watching a cow get milked, and eating dinner earlier than you ever would at home.

The classic Lancaster loop pairs Strasburg's rail attractions with hands-on farm experiences and a morning at Lancaster Central Market, where local vendors have sold produce, meats, and baked goods for generations. Kids tend to love the mix because nothing lasts too long: a train ride, a farm tour, an ice cream stop, then a theme park built at exactly their scale in Dutch Wonderland. Build the days loosely and leave room for the unplanned farm stand or pretzel bakery you pass on the road.

Lancaster suits families with younger children best, especially ages three to twelve, and it works well for multigenerational trips because the pace is gentle and most sights sit close together. One planning note: many Amish-owned businesses close on Sundays, so schedule farm tours and market time on other days. Spring and fall bring mild weather and thinner crowds, while summer adds the full slate of outdoor attractions. Whatever the season, bring an appetite and a cooler for the ride home.

Attractions in Lancaster, PA

Strasburg Rail Road

Day 1 · Morning · 9:30 AM – 12:00 PM · $26/adult, $18/child (est.)

Ride a 45-minute round-trip behind a century-old steam locomotive through Amish farmland. Book the 10:00 AM departure to beat the crowds; the open-air car is best for photos. About a 20-minute drive east of downtown Lancaster.

The Amish Village

Day 1 · Afternoon · 12:30 PM – 2:30 PM · $14/adult, $9/child (est.)

A 10-minute drive from Strasburg, this 12-acre site includes a guided tour of an 1840s Amish farmhouse, a blacksmith shop, and a working farm with animals the kids can meet. Allow about 2 hours.

Kitchen Kettle Village, Intercourse

Day 1 · Evening · 3:30 PM – 6:30 PM · Free entry; dinner ~$15–25/person (est.)

Drive 10 minutes north to Intercourse for 40+ local shops and an early dinner. Grab whoopie pies and fresh fudge for the road, then head back toward Lancaster (about 15 minutes).

Dutch Wonderland

Day 2 · Morning · 10:00 AM – 1:00 PM · $55/person; under 3 free (est.)

Arrive at opening (usually 10:00 AM) and head straight to the back of the park to ride before lines build. With 35+ rides built for kids, plan a full morning; the Duke's Lagoon water area opens midday in summer. The park is on the east side of Lancaster, about 10 minutes from downtown.

Turkey Hill Experience, Columbia

Day 2 · Afternoon · 2:00 PM – 4:00 PM · $13/person (est.)

A 20-minute drive west to Columbia for this interactive ice cream museum — you design your own flavor, star in a mock commercial, and taste unlimited samples. Great air-conditioned break. Alternatively, if it's a Tuesday, Friday, or Saturday, swap in Lancaster Central Market downtown for lunch among the food stalls.

Dinner in Downtown Lancaster

Day 2 · Evening · 5:30 PM – 8:30 PM · Dinner ~$15–30/person; Sight & Sound ~$90/adult (est.)

Drive back into the city (20 minutes) for dinner, then an optional evening show at Sight & Sound Theatres (about 15 minutes east in Strasburg) — its massive stage productions are a Lancaster signature, but book weeks ahead in season.
